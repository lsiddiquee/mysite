SURFACE:
CloakCode Part 3 blog hero and social preview. Visually consistent with the Part 1 and Part 2 heroes
(content/assets/your-copilot-is-waiting-hero.jpg, content/assets/the-standalone-gateway-hero.jpg) —

workstation-to-phone scene, and it has no dominant oversized smartphone.

STORY:
A host, a dev container, and a WSL distro are three separate islands, each sealed inside its own
boundary with its own private loopback loop that cannot see the others. In the middle stands the
gateway as a single small building with two clearly different doors: a narrow operator door kept on
loopback and reached only through a shielded private tunnel, and a wider provider door that is
encrypted and certificate-pinned for the editors. Every crossing between islands is one deliberate,
narrow forwarded line — never a wide-open wall.

IMAGE PROMPT:
A bold editorial vector illustration for a technology blog banner, 16:9 landscape composition, drawn
as a clean schematic network map seen straight on.

Three distinct bounded environment boxes sit around the frame — one upper left, one lower left, one
lower right — each a rounded rectangle with a thick, closed, clearly sealed outline, representing a
host machine, a dev container, and a WSL distro. Each box holds one simple abstract editor screen
with a few plain code bars, and each box has its own small circular loop arrow curled tightly inside
its own border: a private loopback that never leaves its box. The three boxes visibly do not touch or
overlap, and there is clear empty space between them so it reads that they cannot see each other.

In the center stands one small, solid gateway building: a simple flat-front structure with a plain
roof and two obviously different doors on its face. The left door is narrow and tall, set back behind
a short shielded passage — a thick tube or covered corridor drawn like a protective sleeve with a
small shield plate over its mouth — and only one single thin line enters through it. The right door
is wider, drawn with a bold outline, and carries a small badge with a shield-and-ribbon certificate
mark plus a tiny padlock, marking it as the encrypted, pinned door.

From each of the three environment boxes, exactly one thin line leaves through a small notch or gate
in its border — a deliberate narrow crossing, not a gap in a wall — and travels to the gateway. The
two editor boxes' lines terminate at the wide certificate-badged right door. The third line arrives
at the shielded left door through the covered passage. Every line is thin, straight, and orthogonal
with clean right-angle bends, like a routing diagram. Small solid dots mark each crossing point so
the forwarded hops are countable.

The story should be instantly understandable at thumbnail size: three sealed islands, each with its
own private loop, all reaching one central gateway through single narrow guarded crossings, and that
gateway has two visibly different doors.
