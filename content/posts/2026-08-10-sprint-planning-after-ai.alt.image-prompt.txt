Why this concept exists. The caliper concept renders correctly and still fails, because it has to be
explained. A caliper is an obscure instrument, and "jaws closed to zero width means the effort spread
it measured collapsed" only unlocks after reading the story-points section of the post. A hero has
about one second, at card size, on somebody who has not read a word. So this concept keeps the
argument and throws away every object that needs decoding.

The legibility test, applied. Describe the finished picture to somebody with no context: "a big
floodlight is shining on an empty patch of ground, and the actual pile-up is sitting right next to it
in the dark, with nothing pointed at it." Ask what it means. The available answers are "they are
watching the wrong spot", "the light is aimed at nothing", and "nobody is looking at the real
problem". All three are the post's thesis, and none of them require the article. Every object in the
frame passes the same test on its own: a floodlight's purpose is obvious on sight, a bright empty
circle is obvious, and a mass of identical blocks crammed against a gap too narrow to pass is obvious
as a jam. Nothing here is an instrument you have to recognise.

Distinct from its neighbours. It shares no motif with the CloakCode product series: no workstation,
laptop, monitor, phone, QR glyph, network map, house, gateway, door, shield, certificate badge,
padlock, or rounded-rectangle machine boundary. The two neck walls are plain walls with a gap, never
a gate, barrier arm, or turnstile. It must also read as a different picture from the cognitive-tax
hero, which owns overload: nothing here tears, bursts, strains, or arrives unbounded, and no person
appears. Palette-wise it is the only dark-field image on the blog; the caliper sibling is plum,
chartreuse, and sage on pale grey, the CloakCode series is coral, mint, and cyan on warm off-white,
and the cognitive-tax hero is cobalt, amber, and vermilion on newsprint. Keep those out of the
positive prompt and let the closed palette below enforce the difference.

The dominant subject is one bolted floodlight blazing a wide flat pool of light onto empty ground.
The secondary subject is the jam sitting beside it, in the dark, with nothing aimed at it.

STORY:
Engineering planning still schedules around a constraint that has moved. Story points priced an
effort spread that collapsed. Dependency ordering was insurance against rework that got cheap. The
frozen API contract was a scheduling instrument, not a correctness one. Underneath all three is one
sentence: the thing being watched is not the thing that is stuck.

That sentence is the whole picture, stated with objects a five-year-old could name. A lamp is
pointing at bare ground. The pile-up is next to it. Nothing is pointing at the pile-up.

The second idea from the post, that the slowest stage governs arrival however fast the first one
runs, comes free from the geometry rather than from a second subject: there is exactly one narrow
point in the frame, everything is stopped at it, and the ground past it is empty. No lanes, no
chart, no third element.

The load-bearing relationship is that the light contains nothing and touches nothing. A model will
quietly correct that into a sensible picture of a floodlight illuminating the problem, which is the
same failure the sibling concept had to be defended against, so it is stated five independent ways.
First, the lit pool is described as containing nothing at all. Second, no block touches or crosses
the pool's edge, and a clear stretch of unlit ground separates the two. Third, the prompt says
outright that no light of any kind falls on the jam: no second pool, no spill, no rim light, no
glint. Fourth, the lamp is bolted and braced so it cannot swivel, with its head tipped away from the
jam. Fifth, the negative prompt repeats all of it. The lamp must never be shown aimed right, turning,
tilting toward the jam, or connected to it by a beam, line, cable, arm, or dashed path.

The queue's side is stated the same way the sibling's is, because the first render of that concept
put the stopped units past the blockage, which reads as work that got through and then parked. Flow
runs left to right, so the jam packs against the LEFT face of the neck, and the ground from the neck
to the right edge of the frame is bare.

The jam has to read as a jam without becoming the overload picture. It is dense and crowded, because
that is what makes it legible at thumbnail size, but it is bounded: it ends well inside the frame, it
is not cropped by any edge, nothing tears or bursts, nothing sprays, and nothing is still arriving.
Crowded and stopped, not flooding.

Every trace of remedy stays out of frame. Nothing widens the neck, no second lamp arrives, no arrow
reroutes anything, there is no checkmark, no after-state, and no second panel. The picture is one
uncorrected mistake held still.

At thumbnail size the frame carries three roles. A dark field. One blazing butter-yellow pool on the
left with absolutely nothing in it. And a dense persimmon mass on the right, jammed at a narrow gap,
lit by nothing.

LEFT of centre stands the dominant object: a tall floodlight on a mast, unmistakably a work lamp. A
slim cool concrete grey mast with crisp black outlines rises from a wide flat black foot that is
bolted to the ground line with four visible bolt heads and braced on both sides by two solid diagonal
struts, so the whole structure is obviously fixed and immovable and cannot swivel. It rises to about
two thirds of the frame height and carries one plain boxy lamp head: a simple wide rectangle tipped

From that yellow face falls one enormous flat cone of light: a hard-edged butter yellow trapezoid,
narrow where it leaves the lamp and widening steeply downward, landing on the ground line as a broad

largest single area of flat colour in the picture. It is drawn as one solid shape with a crisp black
outline and a hard straight edge all the way round: it is not a glow, not a gradient, not a haze, not

That lit pool is completely empty. Nothing stands in it, nothing lies in it, and nothing crosses it:
no block, no part of a block, no fragment, no object of any kind. It is a bright clean patch of bare

RIGHT of the light, out on the plain dark navy ground, sits the actual jam, and it is unmistakable.
The work units are identical persimmon orange-red blocks: plain solid rectangles with crisp black

one another. The mass is dense, crowded and obviously stuck, and it is the loudest shape on the right
half of the frame.

The jam sits entirely on the LEFT, approach side of the neck, occupying the ground from about three
fifths to about four fifths of the way across. Nothing has got through: the ground from the neck to
the right edge of the frame is completely bare dark navy, with no block, no part of a block, and no
fragment anywhere on it. Like traffic stopped at a closed lane, the line builds on the near side and

Nothing lights the jam and nothing points at it. The lamp stands well to its left with its head
tipped away, and no beam, cone, ray, line, cable, arm, arrow, or dashed path reaches from the lamp or
from anywhere else toward the jam or toward the neck. No butter yellow touches any block: every block
sits on plain dark navy ground, outside the pool, with no rim light, no second pool, no spill, no
halo, and no glint on it. There is a clear stretch of unlit bare ground, at least two blocks wide,
between the right edge of the butter yellow pool and the nearest block, so the light and the jam
never touch or overlap.

The jam is bounded and finite. It ends cleanly well inside the frame, it is not cropped by any edge,
and nothing is spraying, bursting, tearing, tumbling out, or still arriving from off-frame. There is
exactly one lamp, exactly one cone of light, exactly one pool, exactly one neck, and exactly one jam.

The story should be instantly understandable at thumbnail size: a big floodlight blazing on an empty
